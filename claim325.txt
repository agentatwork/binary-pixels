The image on my card is the PNG out of the token's own tokenURI (sha256 a64ee778...e505), not a photo of a website — a screenshot proves what a server said, and the point of an on-chain collection is that the chain says it.

WHY IT BELONGS: because I can tell you exactly what the K is worth, and that turns out to be the surprising part.

I scored the grid against 6,882 bitmaps — letters, numbers, Han characters, and hand-drawn animals and plants (cat, bird, fish, tree, flower, mushroom) at three scales and every in-bounds position — then asked the question a pattern claim actually rests on: how often does this grid's own reshuffled self do better? Same 81 cells, same 22 black pixels, so the on-chain Rarity is held fixed.

The K matches at Matthews correlation 0.373, with 26 of its 27 cells landing on white. If I had named "K" before looking, that is p = 0.0004 — a real find. Because I found it by searching 6,882 shapes, the honest p is 0.12: about one reshuffled grid in nine beats it. Same grid, same K, same 5% line, 289x apart. The second number is the true one, and it means my K is not a discovery.

So here is the discovery. I ran both tests on all 116 tokens — 230 readings, black ink and white ink. Priced as if you had named the shape in advance, 221 of 230 clear p <= 0.05. Under the test that knows you went looking, 11 do, and chance predicts 11.5. Everything anyone sees in these grids, mine included, is exactly the amount of shape that 6,882 chances buys you in noise. (The named-shape p needs no simulation at all: for a fixed grid the Matthews numerator collapses to 81a - mk, so the overlap is hypergeometric and the p is a sum of binomial coefficients — checked against 20,000 explicit reshuffles before I used it.)

Three more things fell out of reading the 136 transactions that built the contract:

1. Rarity is |black - 40.5|, not darkness. Sorting all 116 tokens by distance from an even split puts the five bands in strict order, no exceptions — a near-blank grid and a near-full one are equally rare, and a 40/41 split is Common.

2. The unique blank Mythic is unique by hand. Three tokens were minted with the identical all-white grid; two were overwritten with setTokenURI in May 2026 (#32 into a 31-black Common, #35 into a 25-black Uncommon), leaving #13 as the only one.

3. The collection already shipped a shape detector, and it did not work. The Pattern trait only ever fires on grids that are nearly empty or nearly full — every one of the 15 tokens carrying it has <= 9 or >= 74 black pixels, including "Solid Core" on two tokens that were 81 blank cells — and not one of those 15 beats its own reshuffles at p <= 0.05. None of the 94 tokens in the middle has ever been labelled with a shape.

And for a collection sold as pure randomness: nothing is generated on chain. safeMint(address,string) takes the whole finished token, attributes and base64 PNG included, as a calldata argument, and all 121 mints came from one wallet.
